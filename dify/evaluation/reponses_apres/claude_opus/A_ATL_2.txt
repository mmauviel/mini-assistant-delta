1. Facts
- Share of flights delayed 15 minutes or more in February: 10.8% in 2023, 8.8% in 2024, 17.6% in 2025 and 11.6% in 2026.
- In February 2026, ATL was below the Delta network average: 11.6 - 15.8 = -4.2 points.
- No year was above the 25% threshold (0 years out of 4), and every cause was at or below the airport's average over all months (late-arriving aircraft: 4.6% against 6.0%, -1.4 points).
- Share of delays Delta controls: between 56% and 60%.
- 53% of "national aviation system" delay minutes happened during thunderstorms or low cloud at the airport.

2. Interpretation
The disruption level is normal, because the share of delayed flights never went above our 25% threshold in any of the 4 years (the highest was 17.6% in 2025). Delays are mostly controllable, since the share of delays Delta controls, between 56% and 60%, is entirely above 50%. Bad weather explains more than half of the air-system delay minutes, with 53% of them happening during thunderstorms or low cloud at the airport.

3. Suggestions
Suggestion 1: Monitor weather alerts and prepare a targeted cancellation plan – in real time, only if delays appear in February
Suggestion 2: Track storm forecasts at arrival times and warn passengers as soon as an alert is issued – in real time, as soon as an alert is issued – 53% of air-system delay minutes
