1. Facts

* The share of flights delayed by 15 minutes or more was 10.8% in 2023, 8.8% in 2024, 17.6% in 2025 and 11.6% in 2026.
* The February delay share at ATL in 2026 was 11.6%, compared with the Delta network average of 15.8% (15.8 - 11.6 = 4.2 percentage points below).
* The disruption level was normal: the share of delayed flights exceeded the 25% threshold in 0 out of 4 years.
* All four delay causes were below their airport averages: airline 4.2% (-0.4 points), late-arriving aircraft 4.6% (-1.4 points), national aviation system 3.1% (-0.6 points), and extreme weather 0.4% (-0.2 points).
* Delta controls between 56% and 60% of delays. Within the national aviation system cause, 53% of delay minutes occurred during thunderstorms or low cloud at the airport.

2. Interpretation
The disruption level is normal because the share of delayed flights never exceeded the 25% threshold in the four February observations. Delays are mostly controllable because the estimated controllable share ranges from 56% to 60%, entirely above 50%. Bad weather explains more than half of national aviation system delay minutes, accounting for 53%.
3. Suggestions
Suggestion 1: Track storm forecasts at arrival times and warn passengers as soon as an alert is issued – in real time, only if delays appear – 53%.
Suggestion 2: Monitor weather alerts and prepare a targeted cancellation plan – in real time, only if delays appear.
